# Channel Analysis and Analytics Framework

Faceless animated science/education channel. English narration (AI voice), Spanish subtitles.
Goal: about $1,000 USD per video from YouTube-native income only (AdSense, in-video sponsorships, YouTube Shopping/affiliates, memberships, Super Thanks/Super Chat).
Prepared 2026-11-09. The channel does not exist yet, so everything below comes from competitor research and public benchmarks. No YouTube Studio data was available.

Labels used: **[S]** = sourced figure (see Sources). **[E]** = estimate from a third-party tool, not confirmed by the channel. **[A]** = my own assumption or calculation.

**Patterns to copy** [A, drawn from the table]:
- **Titles:** "What if…", "Why…", "What happens when…", "The [superlative] [thing] in the universe". Under 60 characters, one curiosity gap, no clickbait the video does not pay off.
- **Thumbnails:** one subject, high contrast, at most 3 words, a consistent brand palette and mascot (as Kurzgesagt does). Make the thumbnail work without the title.
- **Sponsors:** the faceless science category is dominated by a small, repeatable pool of brands: Incogni (46 channels), Brilliant (41), Opera (34), Ground News (31) [S]. The next tier includes Saily, Surfshark, Odoo, TripleTen, Speakly and Monarch [E]. These are the first brands to pitch at about 10K–50K views per video.
- **Cadence:** winners range from 1–2 a month with very high polish (Kurzgesagt) to 2+ a week (Astrum). For a small team, 1 a week of 8–14 minute long-form is the realistic middle.
- **Mix:** large channels earn most of their money outside AdSense, from sponsors or the shop. Because we are limited to YouTube-native income, in-video sponsorships will be the main lever, and AdSense is the floor.

## 2. YouTube Partner Program: eligibility and features (verified on support.google.com)

**Fan funding tier (expanded YPP)** [S]: 500 subscribers and 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, plus either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3M Shorts views in 90 days.
- Unlocks channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels and gifts, and Shopping.

**Ad revenue sharing tier** [S]: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
- Unlocks ad and YouTube Premium revenue.

**Other requirements** [S]: follow the channel monetization policies, be based in an eligible country, turn on 2-Step Verification, link one AdSense for YouTube account (created only through Studio), and accept the Commerce Product Module. Reviews take about a month. After a first rejection you can reapply in 30 days, and after later rejections in 90 days.

**YouTube Shopping affiliate** [S]: since 27 March 2026 it requires only YPP membership (500+ subscribers in the expanded tier; previously 10K+). It runs in 14 countries: the US, South Korea, Philippines, India, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Taiwan, Vietnam, Brazil, and from June 2026 Mexico and Argentina with Mercado Libre. Made-for-Kids content is excluded. **Implication:** the channel's base country decides whether this is available. Mexico and Argentina via Mercado Libre fit a Spanish-subtitle audience.

## 3. AI narration and monetization policy (critical)

### What the policy says
1. **"Inauthentic content" (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025)** [S]. YouTube called it a minor clarification, not a new rule. Mass-produced or repetitive content was never monetizable. The official policy text excludes:
   - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
   - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"
   - Allowed: the "same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different."
   - YouTube's examples of violations include channels that post narrated stories differing only superficially, and slideshows with identical narration [S].
2. **Enforcement is channel-level** [S]. If videos violate the policy, monetization can be removed from the whole channel. Reviewers sample the main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, highest watch-time videos, metadata and the channel description [S].
3. **AI tools are explicitly allowed.** YouTube "allows creators to use AI tools in content to enhance their storytelling," and doing so does not by itself affect monetization [S].
4. **2026 escalation.** In his 2026 letter to creators, Neal Mohan committed to reducing "low quality AI content" using YouTube's anti-spam and anti-clickbait systems [S]. In early 2026 YouTube removed or hid at least 18 large AI-slop channels [S]. Third-party reports describe a demonetization wave against faceless channels that use AI voices with repetitive or near-identical scripts, and say YouTube has auto-labelled undisclosed photorealistic AI since May 2026 [E: AIR Media-Tech, not official].
5. **Altered or synthetic content disclosure** [S, Google blog]: creators must disclose *realistic* content a viewer could mistake for a real person, place or event. Google's own list of examples that need disclosure includes **"synthetically generating a person's voice to narrate a video."** No disclosure is needed for clearly unrealistic content such as animation, for AI used to write scripts, ideas or captions, or for colour and lighting effects.

### Concrete guidance for staying monetizable
- **Tick "Altered or synthetic content = Yes" in Studio on every video with AI narration.** A realistic synthetic voice falls inside Google's own example. The label does not reduce monetization, but failing to disclose is a policy risk [S + A].
- **Use a licensed TTS voice or a voice clone of a consenting team member. Never clone or imitate a real public figure.** Keep the licence or consent on file.
- **Show the human editorial work on screen and in metadata** [A]: an original thesis per video, cited sources in the description, a human-written script (AI-assisted drafts are fine), custom animation rather than stock slideshows, and an on-screen sources card. The channel About page should say "Scripts researched and written by [team]; narration is AI-generated."
- **No templated sameness** [A]: every video needs a unique structure, unique visuals and a distinct narrative. Do not run near-identical "Top 10 facts" series with swapped nouns. Reusing the intro, outro and brand assets is fine.
- **Volume discipline** [A]: no more than about 1–2 long-form videos a week at launch. Bursts of daily AI uploads are the pattern flagged in 2026.
- **Keep a production log per video** (research notes, script drafts, project files, voice licence). If an appeal is needed, the third-party advice is to keep uploading paused, not delete flagged videos, and submit evidence of human input. Reviews take about 3–6 weeks [E].
- **Pre-YPP audit** [A]: before applying, check that the 10 most-viewed and 10 newest videos all pass the question "would this be valuable if a human had narrated it?"
- **Consider recording a human voice later** for flagship videos. It reduces risk and helps sponsors, since some brands avoid synthetic hosts [A].
- **Spanish subtitles** are low-risk and help reach Spanish-speaking audiences. Upload human-reviewed .srt files rather than relying on auto-translation alone [A].

## 4. RPM and sponsorship benchmarks

### AdSense RPM (creator earnings per 1,000 total views, after YouTube's 45% cut)
AIR Media-Tech, 300 channels, May 2025–May 2026, medians [S]:

| Niche | Median RPM | P25–P75 |
|---|---|---|
| **Education & Science** | **$10.22** | $2.31–$19.50 (widest spread; ~77% of views monetized) |
| Gadgets & Tech | $2.33 | $0.70–$3.71 (only ~34% of views monetized) |
| Business & Finance* | $2.01 | $0.73–$5.17 (*3 channels only; Q4 about +60%) |
| Health & Sport | $1.23 | $0.72–$1.53 |

Caveats [A]: blog lists often quote finance RPMs of $20–50, but AIR's real-channel medians are far lower outside Education. RPM depends heavily on geography: US, UK, CA and AU viewers pay most, and Spanish-speaking LATAM viewers pay a fraction of that. English narration aimed at a Tier-1 audience is the right call. Q4 (October–December) pays more and January pays less. **Planning assumption: $4 RPM in the first months, $8–10 at steady state, $15+ only if the audience is concentrated in the US/UK.**

### Sponsorship rates (1of10, 2026) [S]
- Dedicated integration: **$10–$50 per 1,000 expected views**. By niche: education $20–35, tech/SaaS $30–50, finance $35–50+, health $18–30.
- By channel size: 1K–10K subs $50–500 (often product plus fee); 10K–50K $200–1,500; 50K–500K $1,000–10,000.
- Format multipliers against a 60–90 s integration (1.0x): a 30 s mention is 0.5–0.7x, a dedicated video 2–4x.
- Pricing floor = median views of the last 10 videos × niche CPM / 1,000, plus production cost. Open 20–30% above the floor.
- Calibration: Veritasium's estimated sponsor income is about $20–40 CPM, and Sciencephile's about $20–40 CPM on ~206K views [E].

### Other native income [A]
- Shopping/affiliate: $0.5–3 per 1,000 views for education, from book, telescope and kit tie-ins.
- Memberships and Super Thanks: about $0.2–1 per 1,000 views early on. Grows with a loyal community.

## 5. The math: views per video for $1,000

Revenue per video ≈ views/1000 × (AdSense RPM + sponsor CPM × share of videos sponsored + affiliate RPM + fan-funding RPM) [A]

**AdSense only** [A]:
| RPM | Views needed |
|---|---|
| $3 (new channel, mixed geography) | ~333,000 |
| $6 | ~167,000 |
| $10.22 (Education median) | ~98,000 |
| $19.50 (top quartile) | ~51,000 |

**AdSense plus one integrated sponsor per video** [A]:
| RPM + sponsor CPM | Views needed |
|---|---|
| $6 + $20 | ~38,500 |
| $8 + $25 | ~30,300 |
| $10 + $30 | ~25,000 |
| $10 + $35 | ~22,200 |

**By stage** [A]:
| Stage | Typical status | Income per video | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Months 0–3 | Not monetized | $0 | Build library and watch hours. Brilliant/Incogni-type affiliate deals only. |
| 1: Fan-funding tier (500 subs, 3K hours) | Memberships, Super Thanks, Shopping | $10–$100 | Turn on Shopping if the country is eligible. |
| 2: Full YPP, ~10–20K views per video | AdSense ~$4–8 RPM + small sponsors | $100–$600 | e.g. 15K views × $6 = $90 AdSense + a $300 sponsor. |
| 3: ~30–40K views, 25K+ subs | RPM ~$8 + sponsor ~$25 CPM | **~$1,000** | The target is realistic here, with a sponsor in most videos. |
| 4: 100K+ views | $10 RPM + sponsor | $3,000–$5,000 | AdSense alone reaches $1,000. |

**Bottom line** [A]: the realistic path to $1,000 is a **median of about 30K views per video with an integrated sponsor**, or about 100K views without one. Plan for this at roughly months 9–18 if the channel compounds. That timeline is not guaranteed.

## 6. KPI framework (YouTube Studio)

| KPI | Where in Studio | Target (early, then mature) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR | Content > Reach | 4–6% early, 6–10% mature. Education usually runs 3–7%, and search traffic lowers CTR [S] | Packaging quality |
| CTR in first 24–72 h vs channel average | Video > Reach, realtime | At or above the channel median | Whether the browse algorithm picks the video up |
| Retention at 30 s | Video > Engagement > Key moments | ≥70% [A] | Hook quality. A sharp early drop means the title or thumbnail promise does not match the opening [S] |
| Retention at 60 s | same | ≥55–65%. Videos with ≥65% at the first minute show 58% higher AVD [S] | Intro pacing |
| Average percentage viewed | Engagement | 10–20 min videos: 35–45%; 5–10 min: 40–55% [S] | Overall quality |
| Average view duration | Engagement | ≥4.5 min on a 12-min video [A] | Watch hours toward YPP. Also, 8+ min allows mid-rolls |
| Returning viewers % | Audience | ≥20% by month 3, ≥30% mature [A] | Brand loyalty and future memberships |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | Audience / per-video | ≥5 early, 3–8 typical for education [A] | Conversion |
| Browse + Suggested share of traffic | Reach > Traffic sources | >50% by month 4 [A] | Algorithmic reach beyond search |
| RPM / CPM, playback-based CPM | Earn | ≥$6 RPM in year one [A] | Monetization quality |
| Top geographies | Audience | ≥50% US/UK/CA/AU [A] | Drives RPM |
| Revenue per video (all sources) | Earn plus sponsor sheet | Track toward $1,000 | North-star metric |
| Spanish-subtitle usage | Audience > Subtitle language (advanced) | Monitor | Is the Spanish audience growing? |

### Weekly review checklist (every Monday, about 45 min) [A]
1. **Last 7 days:** views, watch hours, net subscribers, and estimated revenue vs. the previous week and vs. YPP progress (500 subs/3K hours, then 1K/4K).
2. **Each video published in the last 28 days:** CTR at 48 h vs. channel median, 30 s and 60 s retention, APV, subscribers per 1K views. Mark each one as outlier, average or underperformer.
3. **Underperformers:** if CTR is low, test a new thumbnail or title with Studio's Test & Compare. If the 30 s drop is high, rewrite the hook template for the next script.
4. **Retention graph:** note the 2 biggest dips and 2 spikes, and log the causes in a "lessons" doc.
5. **Traffic sources:** is the Browse/Suggested share rising? Which external videos are recommending ours? Make topics adjacent to them.
6. **Audience:** returning vs. new, top geographies, when viewers are on YouTube (schedule uploads for this).
7. **Monetization:** RPM trend, sponsor pipeline (pitches sent, replies, booked), Shopping clicks.
8. **Compliance:** synthetic-content label ticked, sources in the description, production log saved, no two recent videos sharing a template.
9. **Pick 1 experiment for next week** (thumbnail style, hook format, length) and write down what it is testing.
10. **Monthly add-on:** update the sponsor rate floor (median of last 10 videos × CPM) and compare against competitors.

## 7. Cadence, length and 90-day launch plan

**Recommendations** [A, informed by competitors]:
- **Long-form:** 1 a week, 9–14 min (above 8 min allows mid-rolls; the edu-explainer sweet spot). Every 4–6 weeks, make one flagship of 15–20 min.
- **Shorts:** 3–4 a week, cut from each long-form video (original edits, not reposts) to feed discovery and subscribers. Do not depend on Shorts revenue.
- **Pillars:** pick 3 topic pillars with high RPM and evergreen demand, e.g. space/physics, the human body/biology, and money/technology science. Avoid kids-directed framing, because Made-for-Kids kills RPM and blocks Shopping.

**90-day plan (launch week = week 1)**

| Weeks | Focus | Deliverables | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-launch (2 weeks) | Brand and compliance | Channel art, mascot, palette, thumbnail template, licensed AI voice, About page with AI disclosure, script template with hook plus sources, production log system, 4 videos in the bank | 4 videos finished; checklist signed |
| 1–4 | Launch and calibrate | 4 long-form (1 a week) + 12 Shorts. A/B test thumbnails on every video. Use Spanish .srt from day one | Baseline CTR, 30 s retention and APV established |
| 5–8 | Double down | 4 long-form; make 2 of them sequels or adjacent topics to the best performer. Rewrite the hook template from retention data. Start a list of 30 sponsor targets (Brilliant, Incogni, Ground News, Saily, Surfshark, Odoo, Speakly, TripleTen…) | 30 s retention ≥65%, CTR ≥4%, ~300–500 subs |
| 9–13 | Scale toward YPP | 5 long-form, including 1 flagship of 15–20 min. Collabs or community posts. Self-audit against inauthentic-content policy. Apply for the fan-funding tier once at 500 subs and 3K hours. Pitch the first affiliate or flat-fee sponsor | 500+ subs, 3K hours (stretch target), first sponsor deal |

**Day 90 targets** [A, aggressive but achievable for a quality animated channel]: 13+ long-form videos, ~500–1,000 subs, 2–4K watch hours, at least one video above 10K views, and a sponsor pipeline ready. Full ad revenue and the $1,000-per-video stage are expected later, in months 9–18.

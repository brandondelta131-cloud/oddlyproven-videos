## Topic details

### 1. Is AI Making You Worse at Thinking? What the Experiments Actually Found
**Hook:** Ten minutes with a chatbot can make you worse at a task once the chatbot is gone. *How* you use it seems to decide whether you learn or not.

Key verified facts:
- About 1,000 Turkish high-school math students were studied. GPT-4 access during practice raised practice grades by 48% (plain "GPT Base") and 127% (guardrailed "GPT Tutor"). Once access was removed, the GPT Base group scored **17% lower** than students who never had it. The guardrails largely removed that harm. Source: Bastani et al., "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics," *PNAS* 122(26) e2422633122, 2025. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nas:journl:v:122:y:2025:p:e2422633122
- Three RCTs with 1,222 US participants found that after brief AI help, solve rates without AI fell. In Experiment 1 the AI group solved 0.57 vs 0.73 for controls, and in the reading task 0.76 vs 0.89. Source: Liu, Christian, Dumbalska, Bakker, Dubey, "AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance," arXiv 2604.04721v2, April 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04721v2
- A surprise test 45 days later: the ChatGPT-study group scored 57.5% vs 68.5% for the traditional study group (p = .002, d = 0.68). Source: Barcaui, "ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch," *Social Sciences & Humanities Open*, Nov 2025, via Stanford SCALE repository. https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/chatgpt-cognitive-crutch-evidence-randomized-controlled-trial-knowledge-retention
- In a survey of 319 knowledge workers (936 examples), higher confidence in GenAI was associated with less critical thinking. Source: Lee et al., CHI '25 (Microsoft Research). https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061

Why it scores well: AI is the dominant tech story and draws tech and EdTech advertisers. Many outlets and podcasts cover it (Science Vs, Prof G Markets, ColdFusion and others all ran "Is AI making us dumber/stupid" episodes; see search results via https://rosetta.to/u/sciencevs/is-ai-making-us-stupid). Most coverage is alarmist, so a nuanced, evidence-based take stands out. Sponsors: learning apps, note-taking/productivity software, AI tools, online courses. It is easy to animate (bar charts, two-group experiments, brain diagrams). Evergreen as long as AI tools exist.

### 2. Can Exercise Make Your Brain Younger? A 12-Month Trial Measured It
**Hook:** A randomized trial put inactive adults on a year of aerobic exercise and measured their "brain age" on MRI.

- 12-month RCT with 130 inactive adults aged 26 to 58. Target: 150 minutes per week of moderate-to-vigorous exercise, including two supervised 60-minute sessions per week. Source: Wan L. et al., "Fitness and exercise effects on brain age: A randomized clinical trial," *Journal of Sport and Health Science*, 2026, via News-Medical, 22 Jan 2026. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260122/Regular-aerobic-exercise-slows-a-key-marker-of-brain-aging-in-midlife.aspx
- Brain age gap (brain-PAD) fell about 0.60 years in the exercise group and rose about 0.35 years in controls. The between-group difference was about 0.95 years. (Same source)
- At baseline, each 1 SD of higher VO2max (about 7 mL/kg/min) went with about 1.83 years lower brain-PAD. This is cross-sectional only. (Same source)
- Caveats: brain-PAD is a surrogate marker, cognition and dementia were not primary endpoints, and only about 62% completed post-intervention imaging. (Same source)

Why: Health and longevity get huge search demand. Sponsor fit is excellent (fitness trackers, supplements with caution, health apps). The topic is evergreen and visual (brain-age clocks, VO2max graphs). The honest framing that the effect is about 1 year, not 10, builds trust.

### 3. Is There Really a Spoon of Plastic in Your Brain?
**Hook:** A 2025 study went viral by claiming our brains hold about a spoon's worth of plastic. Now chemists say the measurement may be partly fooled by fat.

- Original study: Campen et al., "Bioaccumulation of microplastics in decedent human brains," *Nature Medicine*, 3 Feb 2025 (DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03453-1). It reported brain levels above liver and kidney, a rise of about 50% between roughly 2016 and 2024 samples, and up to 10 times more in people with dementia (correlation only). Source: UNM release on EurekAlert. https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1072458
- A November 2025 "Matters arising" letter challenged the study for limited contamination controls and missing validation steps. The Guardian identified 7 challenged studies, and Rauert's team listed 18 studies that ignored false positives from human tissue. Source: Mother Jones (reprinting The Guardian, Damian Carrington), 14 Jan 2026. https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/01/scary-research-findings-microplastics-human-health-flawed/
- Py-GC-MS can confuse fat molecules with polyethylene or PVC. Brain tissue is about 60% fat. (Same source; also Fortune, 24 Feb 2026, https://www.fortune.com/2026/02/24/scientists-microplastics-human-health-studies-joke-obesity)
- Campen's response: "there is no recipe book for how to do this". He says most criticism has been conjectural. (Mother Jones)
- "Clean blood" plastic-removal treatments are advertised at up to £10,000. (Fortune; Mother Jones)

Why: Very high fear and curiosity, and the "science self-correcting" angle is rare and shareable. Sponsors: water filters, kitchenware (careful: avoid implied health claims). Visual: lab-method animations, fat vs plastic signal confusion. Ad suitability needs care (keep it calm, not alarmist).

### 4. What Legal Sports Betting Did to Americans' Finances
**Hook:** Since U.S. states legalized sports betting, economists have been watching credit scores, debts and bankruptcies.

- Hollenbeck et al. (2025, UCLA Anderson) found average credit scores fell 0.8 points in states that legalized. Online access raised the likelihood of bankruptcy by about 10% and debt-collection amounts by 8%, appearing about two years after legalization. Source: NPR/NHPR, "When legal sports betting surges, so do Americans' financial problems," 4 Apr 2026. https://www.nhpr.org/2026-04-04/when-legal-sports-betting-surges-so-do-americans-financial-problems
- A New York Fed staff report (sr1184) found 90+ day delinquencies up about 0.3% overall. Among the roughly 3% who started betting after legalization, they rose more than 10%. (Same source) **[UNCERTAIN]** Authors are not named. Check the NY Fed report directly.
- Bettors' quarterly spending went from under $500 (Dec 2019) to over $1,000 (Jun 2021). (Same source)
- A WSJ (2024) figure: 70% of one gambling company's profits came from under 1% of users. (Same source)

Why: Finance topics attract finance advertisers. Covering sports betting is timely, but **sponsor constraint**: never take gambling sponsors. Fits budgeting apps, credit monitoring, investing education. Visual: maps of legalization, debt curves. **[UNCERTAIN]** The primary Hollenbeck paper was not fetched.

### 5. Ozempic's Puzzle: It Protects Hearts Beyond Weight Loss, but Failed in Alzheimer's
**Hook:** The same drug cut heart attacks in a way weight loss doesn't explain, and then failed in two big Alzheimer's trials.

- SELECT analysis (17,604 adults with cardiovascular disease, no diabetes, 41 countries): a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events (MACE) across all baseline adiposity levels. Early weight loss did not predict benefit, and waist reduction explained about one-third. Source: Deanfield et al., *The Lancet* 406:2257–2268, online 22 Oct 2025, via News-Medical, 11 Feb 2026. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260211/Semaglutide-lowers-cardiovascular-events-even-without-major-weight-loss-researchers-report.aspx
- The evoke and evoke+ phase 3 trials (oral semaglutide 14 mg, early Alzheimer's, 566 sites in 40 countries) showed no meaningful benefit on CDR-SB. Some inflammation biomarkers improved. Source: Cummings et al., *The Lancet* 2026 (DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00459-9), via News-Medical, 22 Mar 2026. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260322/Oral-semaglutide-fails-to-slow-early-Alzheimere28099s-decline-in-two-phase-3-trials.aspx
- **[UNCERTAIN]** Participant numbers and effect sizes for evoke were not in the fetched article.

Why: GLP-1 drugs are a massive search topic and draw pharma and health advertisers (high CPM). Evergreen value is lower because the field moves fast. Sponsor fit is tricky: avoid telehealth and compounded-drug sponsors because of medical-advice risk.

### 6. Your Brain Can Sleep in Pieces, and Scientists Just Triggered It on Purpose
**Hook:** In sleep-deprived mice, forcing "local sleep" in parts of the awake brain restored performance on a memory task.

- In sleep-deprived mice, light-pulsing implants induced 30-minute bouts of rhythmic on/off activity. Stimulated regions showed less slow-wave "sleep pressure" afterward. Mice stimulated on both sides performed like rested mice on a tactile memory test. Source: Driessen et al., *Nature Neuroscience* (DOI 10.1038/s41593-026-02318-9), via News-Medical/NIH, 8 Jun 2026. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260608/Researchers-induce-sleep-like-brain-activity-to-reverse-memory-loss.aspx
- The effect depended on the on/off pattern itself, not on reduced firing. (Same source)
- NREM sleep is about 80% of adult sleep. The lab (Chiara Cirelli, UW–Madison) plans human tests with transcranial stimulation. (Same source)
- Caveat: mice only, invasive, and the headline "reverse memory loss" overstates it. (Same source)

Why: Sleep is a huge evergreen interest. Sponsors include sleep trackers, mattresses, and sleep and meditation apps. It is visual (brain-wave animations, "local sleep" patches lighting up).

### 7. Fusion Hits 150 Million Degrees: How Close Is Real Fusion Power?
**Hook:** A private machine just ran hotter than the Sun's core, and its maker promises grid power by 2028.

- Helion's Polaris (7th-generation prototype) reached a 150 million °C plasma. The company says it is the only private machine to use deuterium-tritium fuel. Its previous record was 100 million °C. Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire, 18 Feb 2026. https://www.ans.org/news/article-7765/fusion-roundup-helion-sets-temperature-record-inertia-raises-450m
- Helion has tentatively targeted a 50 MW or larger plant by 2028 (Microsoft PPA) but did not reconfirm this in the new announcement. Orion site construction began in July 2025 in Malaga, WA. (Same source)
- Inertia Enterprises raised $450M in a Series A to commercialize LLNL laser-fusion work. NIF achieved ignition in Dec 2022. (Same source)

Why: Big curiosity, perfect for animation (plasma, magnets, lasers). Tech and energy sponsors are moderate. Evergreen value is moderate because the story changes quickly.

### 8. The Atlantic's "Conveyor Belt" May Slow by Half This Century
**Hook:** A new statistical method says the current that warms Europe may weaken about 51% by 2100, which is much more than earlier models suggested.

- Portmann, Swingedouw, Khattab and Chavent, "Observational constraints project a ~50% AMOC weakening by the end of this century," *Science Advances* 12(16), 15 Apr 2026. Projected slowdown is 43% to 59%, with a best estimate of about 51% vs 1850–1900. Source: Live Science, 21 Apr 2026. https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/climate-change/nations-need-to-prepare-now-key-atlantic-ocean-current-is-much-closer-to-collapse-than-scientists-thought
- Ridge-regularized regression cut prediction error by 79%. Continuous AMOC monitoring began only in 2004. (Same source)
- Experts disagree on which projections are most likely (Laura Jackson, UK Met Office). (Same source)

Why: Viral and visual (ocean-current animations). Climate content tends to get lower ad rates and fewer sponsors.

### 9. The Most Distant Galaxy Ever Confirmed, and Why It Surprised Astronomers
**Hook:** JWST confirmed a galaxy seen just 280 million years after the Big Bang, and it is far brighter than expected.

- MoM-z14 has a spectroscopic redshift of 14.44, beating JADES-GS-z14-0 (14.18). It is seen about 280 million years after the Big Bang. Source: Live Science, on Naidu et al. (2026), "A Cosmic Miracle…," *The Open Journal of Astrophysics* vol. 9 (peer-reviewed Jan 2026). https://www.livescience.com/space/astronomy/previously-unimaginable-james-webb-telescope-breaks-own-record-again-discovering-farthest-known-galaxy-in-the-universe
- It is about 240 light-years across, roughly 400 times smaller than the Milky Way, and nitrogen-rich. (Same source)
- Naidu: "it looks nothing like what we predicted." (Same source)

Why: Space content draws strong views and is ideal for animation, but RPM and sponsor value are low. Best used as a reach or Shorts topic. **[UNCERTAIN]** Check whether a newer record has been announced since January 2026.

### 10. Did Scientists Really Bring Back the Dire Wolf?
**Hook:** Three white wolves carry 20 gene edits. Are they dire wolves?

- 20 edits across 14 genome locations (15 matching dire wolf sequences). The dire wolf genome differs from the grey wolf's by about 12 million DNA letters. Source: Ewen Callaway, Nature (via Scientific American), 4 Aug 2025. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-scientific-debate-over-colossals-de-extinct-dire-wolves
- The IUCN canid expert group rejected calling them dire wolves or proxies. (Same source)
- Colossal is valued at about $10B and predicts mammoth-like elephants as early as 2028. (Same source)

Why: Strong curiosity, but the news is more than 12 months old and RPM is low. Keep as a biology filler or a Shorts topic.

## PILOT RECOMMENDATION: #1, "Is AI Making You Worse at Thinking? What the Experiments Actually Found"

**Why this one for the pilot**
- **Highest combined score (24/25).** It sits in the most-searched tech topic of 2026 and attracts tech, EdTech and productivity advertisers and sponsors.
- **Timely and still developing.** A peer-reviewed version of the Liu/Christian study was presented in Oct 2026 (UC Berkeley release, 9 Oct 2026), so it is fresh news.
- **Defines the channel's brand.** The topic is surrounded by alarmist takes (for example "MIT study: results are terrifying"). A calm "here is what was measured, here are the limits" video demonstrates the positioning.
- **Actionable payoff.** The evidence suggests *hints, not answers*, which gives viewers a useful takeaway (good for retention and shares) and a natural sponsor bridge (learning tools).
- **Animation-friendly with no footage of people needed.** Two-group experiment diagrams, bar charts, a "practice vs exam" reversal graphic, and EEG connectivity maps.
- **Bilingual appeal.** Education and AI are of global interest, which suits Spanish subtitles.

### Pilot fact sheet (scriptwriter-safe)

1. **Turkish math field experiment (peer-reviewed).** Nearly 1,000 high-school students. GPT-4 during practice raised practice grades by **48% (GPT Base)** and **127% (GPT Tutor)**. Once access was removed, GPT Base students scored **17% lower** than students who never had access. The guardrails "largely mitigated" the harm. Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı, Mariman, *PNAS* 122(26) e2422633122, 2025 (EconPapers lists 2025-07-02). https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nas:journl:v:122:y:2025:p:e2422633122
2. **Why GPT Tutor helped:** it gave hints rather than answers and included teacher input. GPT Base students "seemed to rely on the software to solve problems". Quote from Hamsa Bastani: "We're really worried that if humans don't learn, if they start using these tools as a crutch and rely on it." Knowledge at Wharton, 27 Aug 2024. https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education
3. **"10 minutes" RCTs (2026).** Three randomized trials with 1,222 US participants (Prolific), using GPT-5 as the assistant. After AI was removed, the AI group solved fewer problems: **0.57 vs 0.73** (fractions, Exp 1), **0.71 vs 0.77** (Exp 2), **0.76 vs 0.89** (reading, Exp 3). Sessions lasted about 10 to 15 minutes. Liu (CMU), Christian & Dumbalska (Oxford), Bakker (MIT), Dubey (UCLA), "AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance," arXiv 2604.04721v2, Apr 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04721v2
4. **Giving up more.** AI-group skip rates were higher: 0.20 vs 0.11 (Exp 1) and 0.08 vs 0.01 (Exp 3). In Exp 2 the difference (0.10 vs 0.07) was **not significant**. Say "in two of three experiments." (Same source)
5. **Answers vs hints.** In Exp 2, 61% mainly asked for direct answers and 27% asked for hints. Direct-answer users solved **0.65** vs **0.76** for hint users and **0.77** for controls. **Caveat: cross-sectional and self-reported, not proven causal.** (Same source)
6. **Peer review and press.** UC Berkeley's release (9 Oct 2026) says the peer-reviewed paper was presented at the Conference on Language Modeling. Brian Christian: "We showed, ironically, that they are often helping us in ways that are kind of unhelpful." Via News-Medical. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261009/Brief-AI-assistance-may-impair-independent-problem-solving-and-persistence.aspx **[UNCERTAIN]** The exact name of the COLM proceedings paper was not verified.
7. **45-day retention RCT.** 120 undergraduates were randomized to ChatGPT or traditional study. On a surprise test 45 days later they scored **57.5% vs 68.5%** (p = .002, d = 0.68). Barcaui, "ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch," *Social Sciences & Humanities Open*, Nov 2025. https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/chatgpt-cognitive-crutch-evidence-randomized-controlled-trial-knowledge-retention **[UNCERTAIN]** The reported t(83) implies about 85 analyzed participants, not 120, so say "about a hundred students" or check the paper.
8. **MIT "Your Brain on ChatGPT" EEG study (preprint, not peer-reviewed).** 60 students aged 18 to 39 were split into ChatGPT, Google, and no-internet groups writing SAT-style essays. The no-tool group showed the strongest brain connectivity and ChatGPT users the least. The no-internet group could quote its own essays better. Scientific American, 25 Jun 2025. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/does-using-chatgpt-really-change-your-brain-activity
9. **Important nuance from that study.** More connectivity is not automatically better (it could mean overload). Only 18 people completed the final phase. Lead author Nataliya Kosmyna herself warned against overinterpreting the results. (Same source) Use this to debunk "AI rots your brain" headlines.
10. **Timing matters (CHI '26).** 393 adults wrote a policy essay. Under **time pressure (10 min)**, early LLM access helped (score 3.80 vs 2.75 with no LLM). With **enough time (30 min)**, the pattern reversed: late or no LLM access scored best (5.77 and 5.76 vs 4.51 for early access). Continuous access lowered recall (2.03 vs 2.99). Zhi, Kumar, Lee, arXiv 2603.08849, Mar 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08849
11. **Knowledge workers.** A survey of 319 workers (936 examples): higher confidence in GenAI was associated with *less* critical thinking, and higher self-confidence with more. AI shifts thinking toward "verification, response integration, and task stewardship." Lee, Sarkar et al., CHI '25, Microsoft Research. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061 (This is self-reported survey data, not an experiment.)
12. **Honest limits to state in the video.** The tasks were narrow (fractions, SAT reading, short essays), the sessions short, participants mostly online or student samples, and long-term effects are untested. Sources: Liu et al. limitations (arXiv 2604.04721v2) and SciAm critiques (above).

**Suggested script spine:** viral claim ("AI is making us dumb") → the practice-vs-exam reversal (Bastani) → "10 minutes is enough" (Liu et al.) → but *how* you use it matters (hints vs answers, timing) → what the MIT EEG study does and doesn't show → 3 evidence-based rules (try first, ask for hints, then check) → sponsor bridge (learning/productivity tool).

**Don't say:** "AI damages your brain," "MIT proved…," or "permanent memory loss." None of these is supported by the fetched sources.
